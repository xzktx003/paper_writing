\begin{abstract}
Sparse mixture-of-experts (MoE) models expand parameter capacity while routing each token through only a small expert subset. Post-routing compression can reduce inference cost further, but most methods retain a binary execution space: each routed expert either runs in full or contributes nothing. We study a richer full-or-light space in which experts denied full computation still execute a low-cost Light Subnet. This changes the allocation objective. Once a nonzero light response is available, a full expert's contribution no longer equals the marginal value of promoting that expert from light to full. We formalize this marginal value as \emph{Promotion Utility} and decompose it into a full-response contribution term and a light-to-full \emph{Fidelity Gap}. Our framework, \method, combines \emph{Promotion-Aware Allocation} (\PAA) with \emph{Observed-Mass Rescaling} (\OMR). \PAA\ estimates Promotion Utility before full execution and uses cumulative Top-$P$ assignment to adapt the number of Full Experts to each token. \OMR\ rescales the aggregated full/light response using the Observed Mass estimated from offline \emph{Prefix Coverage}. We prove that omitted Promotion Utility controls a residual upper bound, that cumulative Top-$P$ is the minimum-cardinality equal-cost allocation for a target proxy coverage, and that rescaling error is determined by proportional mismatch divided by Observed Mass. Under approximately aligned pruning budgets, \method\ improves the seven-task average over ACE by 2.83 and 2.81 points at 50\% and 60\% effective FFN pruning on Qwen3, with smaller positive margins on Qwen3.6 and Gemma4. These results motivate direct validation of Prefix Coverage, selection quality, and rescaling error under matched compute.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Sparse mixture-of-experts (MoE) models scale parameter capacity while controlling per-token computation by routing each input to a small subset of feed-forward experts \citep{shazeer2017outrageously,lepikhin2021gshard,fedus2022switch}. This sparse activation design underlies modern language models with fine-grained routed experts, shared experts, and top-$k$ gates \citep{dai2024deepseekmoe,jiang2024mixtral,yang2025qwen3,gemmateam2026gemma4}. Yet sparsity largely stops at expert selection. Once the router activates an expert, inference typically evaluates its complete feed-forward network. Post-routing compression can avoid selected expert executions, but the resulting decision is usually binary: a routed expert either runs in full or contributes nothing to the current token \citep{lu2024notall,huang2024harder,zhao2025adak,guo2025dynmoe,xie2024moepruner,lasby2025reap,liu2026aimer,liu2026allocmoe}. We refer to this conventional execution space as \emph{full-or-zero}.

The full-or-zero abstraction overlooks a more useful decision: how much computation should each routed expert receive? Even when an expert does not merit full-width execution, a compact subset of its informative channels can still produce a token-specific response. We therefore replace zeroed experts with \emph{Light Subnets} that reuse the pretrained weights and current activations. This creates a \emph{full-or-light} execution space in which every routed expert contributes, while only selected experts pay the cost of full computation.

This richer execution space changes what should be optimized. In full-or-zero inference, executing an expert adds its complete response, so full-response contribution is a natural selection signal. In full-or-light inference, the Light Subnet has already supplied part of that response. The value of full execution is now the residual recovered by upgrading from \Light\ to \Full. We call this marginal value \emph{Promotion Utility}. An expert with a large full response may require no promotion when its Light Subnet is faithful; an expert with a smaller full response may deserve promotion when light execution misses a substantial component. Promotion Utility, rather than full contribution alone, is therefore the allocation objective induced by full-or-light execution.

We introduce \method, a post-routing framework that realizes this objective without retraining the model or changing its router. Offline, \method\ reorders each expert's informative intermediate channels into a contiguous prefix, yielding a structure-aligned Light Subnet while preserving the original Full Expert exactly. It also calibrates two complementary statistics: the \emph{Fidelity Gap}, which estimates the response recovered by promotion, and \emph{Prefix Coverage}, which estimates how much response mass remains observable under light execution. Online, \emph{Promotion-Aware Allocation} (\PAA) combines the routed full-contribution prior with the Fidelity Gap to estimate Promotion Utility. A cumulative Top-$P$ rule then converts the promotion distribution into a token-adaptive Full/Light assignment under the requested budget.

The assigned Full and Light Experts produce a mixed-fidelity response rather than a sparsified sum over Full Experts alone. \emph{Observed-Mass Rescaling} (\OMR) uses the calibrated Prefix Coverage to estimate the Observed Mass of this response and correct its leading scale distortion. \PAA\ and \OMR\ consequently solve the two decisions introduced by full-or-light inference: \PAA\ determines where full computation has the highest marginal value, and \OMR\ calibrates the aggregate produced by the resulting fidelity mixture. Figure~\ref{fig:overview} presents the complete offline-to-online pipeline.

Our main contributions are as follows.
\begin{itemize}
  \item We formulate post-routing MoE inference as full-or-light allocation and identify Promotion Utility---the response recovered by a light-to-full upgrade---as its governing objective.
  \item We develop \method, which combines structure-aligned Light Subnets, \PAA\ for token-adaptive Full/Light assignment, and \OMR\ for Observed-Mass-based output correction, while preserving the pretrained router and Full Expert functions.
  \item We connect Promotion Utility to the mixed-fidelity residual, prove the minimum-cardinality property of cumulative Top-$P$ promotion under equal upgrade costs, characterize \OMR\ error, and evaluate the quality--computation trade-off across three MoE architectures and multiple budgets.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Efficient MoE inference and expert selection.}
Sparse MoE models limit per-token computation by routing each input to a small expert subset \citep{shazeer2017outrageously}. GShard, Switch Transformer, and ST-MoE establish this paradigm through distributed sharding, simplified routing, and stable sparse training \citep{lepikhin2021gshard,fedus2022switch,zoph2022stmoe}. Subsequent systems adapt routed computation to token difficulty, router confidence, layer sensitivity, or a global budget \citep{huang2024harder,zhao2025adak,guo2025dynmoe,liu2026allocmoe}. These methods primarily decide which experts enter the active set. Post-routing approaches instead retain the pretrained router and reduce the active set using routing frequency, activation statistics, or estimated contribution \citep{lu2024notall,xie2024moepruner,lasby2025reap,liu2026aimer}. Their execution decision is typically full-or-zero. \method\ expands the action space after routing: every selected expert remains active, but each executes as either a Full Expert or a Light Expert. ACE \citep{xu2026aceadaptivecalibrationfreeexpert} supplies the structural full-contribution prior used in our experiments; \PAA\ combines this prior with upgrade necessity and ranks experts by the marginal value of promotion.

\paragraph{Intra-expert compression and multi-fidelity execution.}
Intra-expert compression reduces the cost of each expert rather than the number of experts executed. One-shot pruning reconstructs compressed responses or compensates for removed channels while retaining matrix-friendly structure \citep{frantar2023sparsegpt,an2024flap}; MoE-specific methods allocate nonuniform expert capacity, exploit layer-wise redundancy, or combine expert-aware quantization with online reduction \citep{bai2025diep,huang2025mixture}. These approaches generally install one compressed representation at a fixed fidelity. Conditional-computation models instead expose several computation levels: CoLT5 assigns expensive feed-forward and attention paths to selected tokens \citep{ainslie2023colt5}, and Mixture of Nested Experts learns nested expert sizes along a compute--accuracy frontier \citep{jain2024mixture}. \method\ brings multi-fidelity execution to frozen, pretrained MoE experts. A function-preserving channel permutation exposes a contiguous Light Subnet inside every Full Expert, allowing fidelity to be assigned independently for each routed expert and token.

\paragraph{Information preservation and output rescaling.}
Compression changes both the magnitude and direction of the model response. Reconstruction-based pruning and bias compensation adapt the retained computation after parameters or channels are removed \citep{frantar2023sparsegpt,an2024flap}, while token reorganization preserves low-priority visual information by fusing representations instead of deleting them \citep{liang2022evit}. In expert skipping, gate renormalization rescales the surviving expert outputs; the skipped experts contribute no token-specific observation. \method\ retains a partial response from every routed expert through Light execution. This makes output correction an observed-mass problem: \PAA\ decides which partial responses should be upgraded, and \OMR\ rescales the resulting full/light aggregate using calibrated Prefix Coverage. The separation of promotion and rescaling is specific to a mixed-fidelity execution space and distinguishes \method\ from both expert removal and static compressed-expert replacement.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Overview}

\method\ converts a routed MoE layer from binary expert execution into token-adaptive Full/Light execution. Consider a layer with $N$ experts indexed by $i\in\{1,\ldots,N\}$. For token state $x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$, where $d$ is the model hidden dimension, the pretrained router selects a set $\Rset(x)$ of $k$ experts and produces nonnegative normalized gates $g_i(x)$ with $\sum_{i\in\Rset(x)}g_i(x)=1$. Let $f_i(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$ be the Full Expert output and $y^*(x)$ the corresponding routed output:
\begin{equation}
  y^*(x)=\sum_{i\in\Rset(x)} g_i(x)f_i(x).
  \label{eq:full-output}
\end{equation}

For each routed expert, a fidelity variable $M_i(x)\in\{0,1\}$ chooses between the Full Expert ($M_i=1$) and a width-$S$ Light Subnet ($M_i=0$); when $x$ is fixed, we write $M_i$ for $M_i(x)$. Here, $d_{\mathrm{ff}}$ denotes the intermediate width of the Full Expert, $S<d_{\mathrm{ff}}$ is the Light Subnet width, and $f_i^S(x)$ denotes its output. Collecting the assignments into the mask $M=(M_i(x))_{i\in\Rset(x)}$, we denote the resulting mixed-fidelity observation by $y_O(x,M)$:
\begin{equation}
  y_O(x,M)=\sum_{i\in\Rset(x)} g_i(x)\big[M_i f_i(x)+(1-M_i)f_i^S(x)\big].
  \label{eq:observed-output}
\end{equation}
We denote its residual relative to full execution by $r(x,M)$:
\begin{equation}
  r(x,M)=y^*(x)-y_O(x,M)
  =\sum_{i:M_i=0}g_i(x)\big[f_i(x)-f_i^S(x)\big].
  \label{eq:residual}
\end{equation}

The residual in Eq.~(\ref{eq:residual}) exposes the quantity that allocation should preserve. Under full-or-zero execution, adding expert $i$ recovers $g_i f_i$. We denote the magnitude of this full contribution by $C_i(x)$:
\begin{equation}
  C_i(x)=g_i(x)\lVert f_i(x)\rVert_2
\end{equation}
Under full-or-light execution, promotion adds only $g_i(f_i-f_i^S)$. We define its magnitude as the Promotion Utility $R_i(x)$:
\begin{equation}
  R_i(x)=g_i(x)\lVert f_i(x)-f_i^S(x)\rVert_2
  \label{eq:promotion-utility}
\end{equation}
With $\epsilon>0$ as a numerical stabilizer, we denote the normalized light-to-full discrepancy by the Fidelity Gap $\eta_i(x)$:
\begin{equation}
  \eta_i(x)=\frac{\lVert f_i(x)-f_i^S(x)\rVert_2}{\lVert f_i(x)\rVert_2+\epsilon}.
  \label{eq:fidelity-gap}
\end{equation}
Up to the stabilizer $\epsilon$, $R_i(x)=C_i(x)\eta_i(x)$. This factorization shows that the benefit of promoting expert $i$ depends on both the magnitude of its Full response and the fraction of that response missed by Light execution. Promotion Utility therefore ranks light-to-full upgrades, whereas full contribution measures only the magnitude of the Full response. Since evaluating $R_i(x)$ exactly would already require both fidelities, \PAA\ estimates its two factors before expert execution.

Figure~\ref{fig:overview} summarizes the two-stage pipeline. Offline Calibration constructs contiguous Light Subnets, estimates expert-level Fidelity Gaps and Prefix Coverage, and maps each target compute budget to a promotion threshold. Online, \PAA\ assigns Full/Light fidelity from estimated Promotion Utility, and \OMR\ rescales the resulting mixed-fidelity aggregate from its estimated Observed Mass.

\subsection{Offline Calibration}

Offline Calibration prepares the structure and expert-level statistics reused by every token. It has three outputs: a contiguous Light Subnet for each expert, the Fidelity Gap and Prefix Coverage statistics, and a budget-specific promotion threshold.

\subsubsection{Structure-Aligned Light Subnet Construction}

For a SwiGLU expert, let $W_{g,i},W_{u,i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{ff}}\times d}$ be the gate and up-projection matrices, and let $W_{d,i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d_{\mathrm{ff}}}$ be the down-projection matrix. Its Full Expert output is
\begin{equation}
  f_i(x)=W_{d,i}\big[\operatorname{SiLU}(W_{g,i}x)\odot (W_{u,i}x)\big].
  \label{eq:swiglu}
\end{equation}
Let $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{cal}}$ be the calibration set, and let $c\in\{1,\ldots,d_{\mathrm{ff}}\}$ index an intermediate channel. We define the routed channel score $r^{\mathrm{act}}_{i,c}$ from its typical activation and its transmission through the down projection:
\begin{equation}
  r^{\mathrm{act}}_{i,c}=
  \mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{cal}}\mid i\in\Rset(x)}
  \left[\left|\operatorname{SiLU}(W_{g,i}x)_c(W_{u,i}x)_c\right|\right]
  \lVert W_{d,i}[:,c]\rVert_2.
  \label{eq:channel-score}
\end{equation}
Let $z_{i,c}(x)=\operatorname{SiLU}(W_{g,i}x)_c(W_{u,i}x)_c$ denote the post-SwiGLU activation of channel $c$. The expectation term $\mathbb{E}[|z_{i,c}(x)|]$ measures its average activation magnitude on tokens routed to expert $i$, while $\lVert W_{d,i}[:,c]\rVert_2$ measures how strongly one unit of that activation is projected into the output space. Their product equals the expected norm of channel $c$'s individual output contribution, $\mathbb{E}[\lVert W_{d,i}[:,c]z_{i,c}(x)\rVert_2]$, and therefore provides a direct ranking score for Light Subnet construction.

Let $\pi_i$ sort channels in descending $r^{\mathrm{act}}_{i,c}$. We apply the same permutation to both input projections and the matching column permutation to the down projection:
\begin{equation}
  W_{g,i}\leftarrow W_{g,i}[\pi_i,:],\quad
  W_{u,i}\leftarrow W_{u,i}[\pi_i,:],\quad
  W_{d,i}\leftarrow W_{d,i}[:,\pi_i].
  \label{eq:channel-reordering}
\end{equation}
Applying one shared permutation across the two input projections and the matching down-projection columns leaves full-width execution unchanged. The first $S$ reordered channels form $\mathcal{C}_i^S=\{\pi_i(1),\ldots,\pi_i(S)\}$ and define
\begin{equation}
  f_i^S(x)=W_{d,i}[:,\mathcal{C}_i^S]
  \left[
  \operatorname{SiLU}(W_{g,i}[\mathcal{C}_i^S,:]x)
  \odot W_{u,i}[\mathcal{C}_i^S,:]x
  \right].
  \label{eq:light-expert}
\end{equation}
Thus each Light Subnet is embedded directly in its Full Expert, reuses the original parameters and token activations, and executes as a contiguous width-$S$ dense GEMM. The construction introduces no retraining and preserves the Full Expert function exactly.

\subsubsection{Calibrating the Fidelity Gap and Prefix Coverage}

\PAA\ needs upgrade information before running both fidelities. We denote the mean Fidelity Gap over calibration tokens routed to expert $i$ by $h_i^{\mathrm{FG}}$:
\begin{equation}
  h_i^{\mathrm{FG}}=
  \mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{cal}}\mid i\in\Rset(x)}[\eta_i(x)].
  \label{eq:calibration-gap}
\end{equation}
Let $N_i$ be the number of calibration tokens routed to expert $i$, let $\ell(i)$ denote its layer, and let $\bar h_{\ell(i)}$ be the mean Fidelity Gap in that layer. Given a shrinkage scale $\tau>0$, let $\lambda_i$ denote the expert-specific shrinkage weight and $\widetilde h_i$ the stored Fidelity Gap estimate:
\begin{equation}
  \widetilde h_i=\lambda_i h_i^{\mathrm{FG}}+(1-\lambda_i)\bar h_{\ell(i)},
  \qquad \lambda_i=\frac{N_i}{N_i+\tau},
  \label{eq:shrinkage}
\end{equation}
The stored $\widetilde h_i$ is the expert-level Fidelity Gap used by \PAA\ to modulate promotion.

\OMR\ uses a distinct statistic. We denote the fraction of offline channel-importance mass covered by the Light Subnet as its Prefix Coverage $c_i$:
\begin{equation}
  c_i=\frac{\sum_{c\in\mathcal{C}_i^S}r^{\mathrm{act}}_{i,c}}
  {\sum_{c=1}^{d_{\mathrm{ff}}}r^{\mathrm{act}}_{i,c}+\epsilon}.
  \label{eq:prefix-coverage}
\end{equation}
The two statistics answer different questions. $\widetilde h_i$ is estimated from paired Full and Light outputs and measures their normalized output-space discrepancy. A larger $\widetilde h_i$ indicates that promotion is expected to recover more of expert $i$'s response, so \PAA\ uses it to rank upgrade utility. In contrast, $c_i$ is the fraction of calibration channel-importance mass contained in the contiguous prefix. A larger $c_i$ indicates that more response mass is expected to remain observable under Light execution, so \OMR\ uses it to estimate the gate-weighted Observed Mass. This separation lets \PAA\ decide where to spend full computation and \OMR\ calibrate the scale of the response retained by Light execution.

\subsubsection{Budget-to-coverage calibration}

For a target budget setting $B$, let $\rho_B$ be the requested effective FFN pruning ratio and $P_B$ the promotion-coverage threshold used online. Each token has $k=|\Rset(x)|$ routed experts. Because every Light Expert still evaluates $S$ channels, the resulting cost depends on how many of these $k$ experts are promoted. We denote the channel-equivalent FFN cost of mask $M$ by $C(M)$ and the incremental cost of one promotion by $\Delta C=d_{\mathrm{ff}}-S$:
\begin{equation}
  C(M)=\sum_{i\in\Rset(x)}\big[M_i d_{\mathrm{ff}}+(1-M_i)S\big],
  \qquad \Delta C=d_{\mathrm{ff}}-S,
  \label{eq:cost}
\end{equation}
The corresponding effective FFN pruning ratio $\rho_{\mathrm{FFN}}(M)$ is
\begin{equation}
  \rho_{\mathrm{FFN}}(M)=1-\frac{C(M)}{k d_{\mathrm{ff}}}.
  \label{eq:prune-ratio}
\end{equation}
For a candidate threshold $P\in(0,1]$, let $M_P(x)$ denote the Full/Light mask produced by cumulative Top-$P$ assignment. For each target $\rho_B$, we search over $P$ on the calibration workload and choose $P_B$ such that
\begin{equation}
  \mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{cal}}}
  \left[\rho_{\mathrm{FFN}}\big(M_{P_B}(x)\big)\right]\approx\rho_B.
  \label{eq:budget-calibration}
\end{equation}
We fix the calibrated map $\rho_B\mapsto P_B$ for evaluation. This map turns a user-facing compute target into the promotion mass that \PAA\ must cover.

\subsection{Online Inference}

\subsubsection{Promotion-Aware Allocation (\PAA)}

For token $x$, the pretrained router returns $\{(i,g_i(x))\}_{i\in\Rset(x)}$. Let $a_i^{\mathrm{ACE}}$ be the stored full-contribution prior for expert $i$ computed by ACE \citep{xu2026aceadaptivecalibrationfreeexpert}. We define the estimated Promotion Utility $\widehat R_i^{\mathrm{PAA}}(x)$ as
\begin{equation}
  \widehat R_i^{\mathrm{PAA}}(x)
  =g_i(x)a_i^{\mathrm{ACE}}\widetilde h_i.
  \label{eq:paa-utility}
\end{equation}
The score is large when an expert contributes strongly to the current token and has a large Fidelity Gap. \PAA\ therefore ranks routed experts by the response expected to be recovered through promotion.

Let $p_i(x)$ denote the normalized promotion score over the routed set:
\begin{equation}
  p_i(x)=\frac{\widehat R_i^{\mathrm{PAA}}(x)}
  {\sum_{j\in\Rset(x)}\widehat R_j^{\mathrm{PAA}}(x)+\epsilon},
  \label{eq:paa-score}
\end{equation}
After sorting $p_i(x)$ in descending order as $p_{(1)}(x)\geq\cdots\geq p_{(k)}(x)$, \PAA\ selects the shortest prefix whose cumulative score reaches $P_B$:
\begin{equation}
  K_B(x)=\min\left\{m\in\{1,\ldots,k\}:\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{(j)}(x)\geq P_B\right\}.
  \label{eq:cumulative-top-p}
\end{equation}
The first $K_B(x)$ experts form $\Fset(x)$ and execute in \Full; the remainder form $\Lset(x)$ and execute in \Light. Concentrated promotion mass reaches $P_B$ with fewer Full Experts, while diffuse mass allocates full computation more broadly. We also retain the router's top-1 expert in $\Fset(x)$.

Every promotion has the same incremental cost $d_{\mathrm{ff}}-S$. The descending prefix therefore reaches $P_B$ with the fewest promotions and the lowest incremental channel-equivalent cost. Appendix~\ref{app:theory} formalizes this property for equal-width Light Subnets.

\subsubsection{Mixed-Fidelity Execution and Observed-Mass Rescaling (\OMR)}

After \PAA\ assigns fidelity, Full and Light kernels evaluate their respective expert sets. With threshold $P_B$ fixed, we write $y_O(x)=y_O(x,M_{P_B}(x))$ for their aggregated token-specific response:
\begin{equation}
  y_O(x)=\sum_{i\in\Fset(x)}g_i(x)f_i(x)
  +\sum_{i\in\Lset(x)}g_i(x)f_i^S(x).
  \label{eq:mixed-aggregate}
\end{equation}
Each Light Expert supplies a partial observation of its Full Expert response. Let $\varepsilon_i(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$ denote the directional mismatch remaining after scaling the Full Expert response by $c_i$. We model the Light Expert output as
\begin{equation}
  f_i^S(x)=c_i f_i(x)+\varepsilon_i(x),\qquad c_i\in[0,1],
  \label{eq:light-model}
\end{equation}
Full Experts contribute unit mass, while Light Experts contribute their calibrated Prefix Coverage. We denote the total gate-weighted Observed Mass by $m_O(x)$:
\begin{equation}
  m_O(x)=\sum_{i\in\Fset(x)}g_i(x)
  +\sum_{i\in\Lset(x)}g_i(x)c_i.
  \label{eq:observed-mass}
\end{equation}
Let $\epsilon_m>0$ be a numerical floor for the rescaling denominator and let $\widehat y(x)$ denote the recovered output. \OMR\ computes
\begin{equation}
  \widehat y(x)=\frac{y_O(x)}{\max(m_O(x),\epsilon_m)}.
  \label{eq:omr}
\end{equation}
\OMR\ rescales the mixed-fidelity output without changing its token-specific direction. Appendix~\ref{app:theory} shows that the remaining error is directional mismatch normalized by Observed Mass for each token.

Full and Light execution retain regular dense GEMM shapes. The online controller remains outside the expert kernels and adds only scalar expert scoring, sorting and accumulating the routed top-$k$ scores, accumulating Observed Mass, and scaling one hidden vector before returning the layer output.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Setup}

We evaluate Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct-2507, Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, and Gemma-4-26B-A4B-it, covering different routing designs, expert granularities, active parameter counts, and backbones \citep{yang2025qwen3,qwenteam2026qwen36,gemmateam2026gemma4}. We measure language-modeling quality with WikiText-2 perplexity at sequence length 2048 \citep{merity2017pointer}. The downstream suite evaluates physical commonsense with PIQA \citep{bisk2020piqa}, grade-school science with ARC-Easy and ARC-Challenge \citep{clark2018arc}, competition mathematics with MATH-500 \citep{hendrycks2021math}, graduate-level science with GPQA-Diamond \citep{rein2023gpqa}, and code generation with HumanEval pass@1 \citep{chen2021evaluating}. The main comparison reports the unweighted seven-task accuracy average, with ARC-Easy and ARC-Challenge counted separately.

Component ablations use Qwen3.5-122B at 10\% and 15\% effective FFN pruning. WikiText-2 is reported separately as perplexity, while the ablation mean averages the six accuracy tasks.

We evaluate target effective FFN pruning ratios from 10\% to 60\% in increments of 10\%. For each target, the budget threshold is calibrated once through Eq.~(\ref{eq:prune-ratio}) and fixed throughout evaluation. All methods use BF16 weights and a common inference stack. The reported ratios denote nominal effective FFN budgets, with realized compute determined by the routed expert distribution.

We compare \method\ with router-score ranking, NAEE \citep{lu2024notall}, MoDES \citep{huang2026modes}, DiEP \citep{bai2025diep}, and ACE \citep{xu2026aceadaptivecalibrationfreeexpert}. Table~\ref{tab:main-results} presents the methods evaluated across all three models and six pruning ratios. Additional Qwen3 comparisons appear in the appendix. ACE skips selected Full Experts, whereas \method\ replaces them with Light Experts, so the main table aligns methods by nominal effective FFN budget rather than end-to-end latency.

\subsection{Main results}

\input{tables/main_results.tex}

Table~\ref{tab:main-results} compares average downstream accuracy across three MoE architectures and six effective FFN pruning ratios. \method\ matches or exceeds ACE at every evaluated model--budget pair. Its advantage becomes most pronounced under aggressive compression: at 50\% and 60\%, \method\ improves over ACE by 2.83 and 2.81 points on Qwen3, 0.81 and 0.80 points on Qwen3.6, and 1.05 and 1.25 points on Gemma4.

The high-compression advantage is consistent across the three architectures. At both 50\% and 60\%, \method\ attains the highest average among all compressed methods for every model. This regime is precisely where full-or-zero pruning removes the largest fraction of routed expert responses, while Full-or-Light execution retains a structured approximation from every routed expert.

The widening margin as the budget tightens supports the central premise of \method: preserving partial routed responses is increasingly valuable when fewer experts can be executed at full fidelity.

Table~\ref{tab:ablation} adds \PAA\ and \OMR\ cumulatively to ACE. The results identify \PAA\ as the main source of recovery: directing the Full-Expert budget toward experts with high Promotion Utility raises mean accuracy from 86.21 to 86.91 at 10\% pruning and from 85.43 to 86.47 at 15\% pruning.

Adding \OMR\ further improves mean accuracy to 87.03 and 86.85, respectively, bringing the 10\% result within 0.01 points of the Dense model. WikiText-2 perplexity improves along the same sequence: 5.41 $\rightarrow$ 5.29 $\rightarrow$ 5.28 at 10\%, and 5.59 $\rightarrow$ 5.34 $\rightarrow$ 5.31 at 15\%. The two stages therefore play complementary roles: \PAA\ determines where full computation yields the greatest recovery, while \OMR\ corrects the aggregate scale of the retained mixed-fidelity response.

\subsection{Efficiency}

Effective FFN pruning is a theoretical compute proxy, not a measurement of wall-clock speedup. We therefore reserve deployment claims for TTFT, TPOT, throughput, and peak-memory measurements under fixed hardware, batch size, sequence length, and kernel implementation. The efficiency analysis must also isolate \PAA\ scoring, \OMR\ rescaling, channel-statistics collection, and Fidelity Gap calibration. Contiguous Light Subnets reduce the kernel family to a small number of fixed full/light GEMM shapes, but their realized benefit still depends on grouped execution, dispatch, and memory traffic. Appendix~\ref{app:pending-tables} specifies the pending measurements.

\section{Limitations and Discussion}
\label{sec:limitations}

The current estimator uses a static expert-level Fidelity Gap. Tokens routed to the same expert can have different approximation errors, so an expert mean may miss the upgrade value of the current token. A low-cost token-conditioned gap predictor could use router state, hidden-state summaries, or partial activations, but its overhead must remain below the saved expert computation.

All experts currently share one light width $S$. Expert redundancy and approximation difficulty can vary strongly, making a uniform width an imperfect fidelity definition. Expert-specific or token-conditioned widths would turn equal-cost prefix selection into utility-per-cost allocation. The minimum-cardinality result would no longer apply directly, but the Promotion Utility objective would remain valid.

\OMR\ is a scalar rescaling rule. It can correct missing scale along the observed direction but cannot reconstruct components orthogonal to the full/light aggregate. Equation~\eqref{eq:rescaling-error} makes this boundary explicit. Low-rank or direction-aware rescaling may reduce mismatch, but any added model must be evaluated against its latency and memory cost.

The ACE comparisons use approximate nominal alignment, some generation settings are not yet unified, and the Gemma4 perplexity pipeline remains unresolved. We therefore treat the cross-method differences as preliminary and do not attribute them solely to the allocation mechanism. The theoretical results characterize the objective and rescaling model, while stronger comparative claims require the matched-compute, causal-ablation, and efficiency evidence specified in Section~\ref{sec:experiments}.

\section{Conclusion}

Full-or-light execution reframes post-routing MoE compression around upgrade value. An upgrade should recover information omitted by the Light Subnet response. \method\ implements this principle with \PAA\ for token-adaptive Full/Light allocation and \OMR\ for output rescaling, while our analysis connects both stages to output error. Under nominally aligned budgets, the seven-task average improves over ACE most clearly at 50\% and 60\% effective FFN pruning across all three evaluated architectures. Matched-compute comparisons and direct Fidelity Gap, selection, and \OMR\ diagnostics remain necessary to determine how much of this advantage comes from the proposed mechanism.

\section{Theoretical Analysis}
\label{app:theory}

\subsection{Promotion residual}

\begin{proposition}[Promotion residual]
For any full/light mask $M$,
\begin{equation}
  \lVert y^*(x)-y_O(x,M)\rVert_2\leq\sum_{i:M_i=0}R_i(x).
  \label{eq:prop-residual}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
Substituting Eq.~(\ref{eq:observed-output}) into Eq.~(\ref{eq:full-output}) leaves only light-executed experts. Applying the triangle inequality gives
\begin{align}
  \lVert y^*-y_O\rVert_2
  &=\left\lVert\sum_{i:M_i=0}g_i(f_i-f_i^S)\right\rVert_2\\
  &\leq\sum_{i:M_i=0}g_i\lVert f_i-f_i^S\rVert_2
  =\sum_{i:M_i=0}R_i.
\end{align}

\subsection{Minimum equal-cost prefix}

\begin{proposition}[Minimum equal-cost coverage]
Assume all experts use the same Light Subnet width $S$. For any nonnegative proxy distribution $p_i$, the descending cumulative Top-$P$ prefix contains the fewest Full Experts among all sets whose proxy mass is at least $P_B$.
\end{proposition}
Let $p_{(1)}\geq\cdots\geq p_{(k)}\geq0$. Among all sets of cardinality $m$, the descending prefix maximizes total mass because replacing any selected lower-ranked element with an unselected higher-ranked element cannot reduce the sum. If the shortest prefix reaching $P_B$ has size $m^*$, no set with fewer than $m^*$ elements can reach $P_B$. Since every promotion costs $d_{\mathrm{ff}}-S$, the prefix also has minimum incremental channel-equivalent cost.

\subsection{Promotion-utility proxy quality}

Define the estimated full contribution as $\widehat C_i(x)=g_i(x)a_i^{\mathrm{ACE}}$. Let $\delta_C,\delta_\eta\in[0,1)$ bound the relative errors of the contribution and Fidelity Gap estimates, respectively. If
\begin{align}
  (1-\delta_C)C_i(x)&\leq \widehat C_i(x)\leq(1+\delta_C)C_i(x),\nonumber\\
  (1-\delta_\eta)\eta_i(x)&\leq \widetilde h_i\leq(1+\delta_\eta)\eta_i(x),
\end{align}
then
\begin{equation}
  (1-\delta_C)(1-\delta_\eta)R_i(x)
  \leq \widehat R_i^{\mathrm{PAA}}(x)
  \leq (1+\delta_C)(1+\delta_\eta)R_i(x).
  \label{eq:proxy-product}
\end{equation}
This decomposition motivates separate correlation and heterogeneity diagnostics for the two estimated factors.

\subsection{OMR Rescaling Identity}

Define the proportional observation mismatch as
\begin{equation}
  \xi(x)=y_O(x)-m_O(x)y^*(x).
  \label{eq:mismatch}
\end{equation}
When $m_O(x)>\epsilon_m$, Eq.~(\ref{eq:omr}) gives
\begin{equation}
  \widehat y(x)-y^*(x)=\frac{\xi(x)}{m_O(x)},
  \qquad
  \lVert\widehat y(x)-y^*(x)\rVert_2
  =\frac{\lVert\xi(x)\rVert_2}{m_O(x)}.
  \label{eq:rescaling-error}
\end{equation}
For $y_O(x)\neq 0$, the scalar minimizing $\lVert\beta y_O(x)-y^*(x)\rVert_2$ is $\beta^*(x)=\langle y^*(x),y_O(x)\rangle/\lVert y_O(x)\rVert_2^2$. When $y_O(x)\approx m_O(x)y^*(x)$, the OMR factor $1/m_O(x)$ approximates this oracle coefficient.
